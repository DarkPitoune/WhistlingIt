Screen one

The daily

Seven things on screen, no scrolling, one thumb. The count and the play button are the only elements above the fold worth looking at.

The bar is four things at once

Position, how much is unlocked, where the next notes fall, and where the crowd usually solves. That's why the ladder, the tries counter and the guess history could all go — they were saying the same thing four times.

The count is the feedback

Get it wrong and 3 notes flips to 4 notes while the bar grows underneath. No error toast, no red banner. The number moving is the "no".

The crowd is a tick, not a sentence

A small blaze mark on the bar shows where most people solve it. Like par on a scorecard — you read it in passing and it costs nothing.

Category and difficulty, one line

Film · Tricky. Enough to frame the guess, not enough to narrow it. Everything else waits for the reveal.

The answer is the hero

Not your score. The title sits in the coloured block at the top, because "what was that?" is the question everyone actually has.

Green solved, indigo missed

The tape

Six blocks, one per try. Blaze wrong, butter skip, green solved. This is the thing that gets pasted into a group chat, so it has to read at 40 px wide.

Screen two

The booth

The record button is the page. Everything else is a short tail underneath it.

Record first, label after

No title field, no category, no consent box in the way. You whistle, then you say what it was. Reversing that order kills contributions.

Detection reports, it doesn't ask

14 notes found. Same bar as the game, showing where the six levels land. One glance to catch a bad cut, one tap to adjust. Nobody places a marker from scratch.

Accepted answers is the real field

Guessing is free text, so this list is the matching logic. Asking "what else would you accept?" gets far better data than calling it aliases.

Difficulty is not a field

Nobody can rate their own whistling. It's computed from the first hundred plays — one footnote, no input.

What came off

The daily went from fourteen elements to seven. Nothing was lost, because most of what came off was the same fact wearing a different hat.

Removed

  • —The pitch-contour waveform
  • —Six level buttons
  • —The wrong-guess history list
  • —"3 tries left" counter
  • —Time labels, 0:00 / 0:13
  • —Back-to-start button
  • —Difficulty as five dots
  • —"Most whistlers crack it on note 4"
  • —The solve histogram
  • —Whistle ↔ original A/B toggle

Where it went

  • →Nowhere. Whistling is a thin, quiet sound; the drawing was decoration.
  • →Into the bar's tick marks and the big count.
  • →Into the tape on the result card.
  • →Implied by the count — six levels, you're on three.
  • →Nowhere. It's an 11-second clip.
  • →Tap the bar, or press play again at the end.
  • →One word next to the category: Film · Tricky.
  • →A single tick on the bar, where par goes.
  • →One line: note 5 · most got it on 4.
  • →Cut. Fun, but it's a second feature wearing a button.
Foundations

The kit

Screen-printed toy: cool periwinkle ground, hard offset shadows instead of soft elevation, and exactly one loud colour so the play button is never in doubt.

Blaze is spent on one thing per screen: the play button, then your marker on the bar. Indigo carries structure and everything locked. The set has a dark twin — this page is already using it if your system is dark.

Motion

  • 380msThe count flips over and the bar grows. The only animation that carries meaning.
  • 340msDragging into the locked stretch shakes the bar. No copy needed — the hatching already says why.
  • 100msEvery chunky button drops onto its own shadow. Physical, cheap, addictive.
  • 1.6sA slow ring pulses off play while audio runs. Whistling is quiet — the screen has to carry the fact that something is playing.

Type — four ways to go

Pick one and the whole page re-skins, phones included. Judge them on the count: it's the one element carrying the personality, and it's the first thing anyone looks at.

Guessing
Free text, no autocomplete.Matched server-side against the accepted-answers list from the booth. Costs a normalisation step; buys a harder game and no spoilers leaking out of a dropdown.
Note cuts
Auto-detected, confirmed in one glance.The booth reports what it found on the same bar the game uses, so a bad cut is obvious without anyone tapping along to a click track.
Timeline
Full track always visible.Unlocked is solid, locked is hatched and un-scrubbable. Seeing how much tune is still hiding is what makes three notes feel tense.
Skip cost
A skip and a wrong guess cost the same.One currency. The tape tells them apart for bragging rights; the rules don't.
Replays
Free and unlimited.The scarce resource is notes, not listens. Charging for replays only punishes bad speakers.
Navigation
No tab bar.Two screens don't need one. The booth is one link in the header, so the daily always opens straight into the game.
Sound
Silence, except the whistle.No win chime, no error buzz. The app's only sound is the thing you're identifying — which also means it works in a meeting.

Five things I'd want your call on

  1. Notes are an uneven currency. In the prototype, three notes is 1.86 s — but note 4 buys only 0.62 s more, while note 7 buys 1.86 s. Should a level be "the next note or +1.5 s, whichever is longer"?
  2. Timezone. One global tune rotating at UTC midnight, or local-midnight per user? The first keeps the crowd tick honest, the second is friendlier.
  3. Late arrivals. Open the app at 23:50 — fresh full round, or the tail of the day?
  4. Accounts. Streaks imply identity. Device-local streak to start, optional account later?
  5. Booth access. Open to everyone with a moderation queue, or invited whistlers only? It decides whether the booth needs a rejection state.
